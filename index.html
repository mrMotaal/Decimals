📌 Idea / Concept: Lesson 5 — Ordering Decimals Using Number Lines & Place-Value Charts

📌 Ordering Decimals Using a Number Line & Place-Value Chart
1 Easton has $\$15.40$. Adeline has $\$15.35$. Gemma has $\$15.43$.
Arrange their money in order from least to greatest. Who has the most money?
✍️ Student Workspace
✓ Step-by-Step Model Solution Place-Value & Number Line Comparison
  • Compare the digits from left to right (Tens $= 1$, Ones $= 5$ are equal for all three).
  • In the tenths place: Adeline has $3$ tenths ($15.35$), while Easton ($15.40$) and Gemma ($15.43$) have $4$ tenths. So $\$15.35$ is the least.
  • Compare hundredths for Easton ($0$) and Gemma ($3$): $0 < 3 \implies \$15.40 < \$15.43$.
  • The order from least to greatest is: $\$15.35, \$15.40, \text{ and } \$15.43$.
Least to Greatest: $15.35, $15.40, $15.43 | Gemma has the most money

📌 Idea / Concept: Lesson 6 — Rounding Decimals Using Number Lines & Place-Value Rules

📌 Rounding to Nearest Whole Number & Nearest Tenth
7 Use the number lines and rounding rules to solve the following real-world rounding problems:
1 The biggest flower in the world (Rafflesia Arnoldii) can weigh up to $6.8\text{ kg}$. Round $6.8$ to the nearest whole number.
2 Round $52.73$ to the nearest tenth.
3 The smallest fish in the world (Paedocypris progenetica) is $7.9\text{ mm}$ long. Round its length to the nearest ones.
✍️ Student Workspace
✓ Step-by-Step Model Solution Number Line Proximity & Rounding Rules
  • (1) $6.8$ is between $6$ and $7$, and is closer to $7$ (since the tenths digit is $8 \ge 5$). So $6.8$ rounds to $\mathbf{7}$.
  • (2) $52.73$ is between $52.7$ and $52.8$, and is closer to $52.7$ (hundredths digit is $3 < 5$). So $52.73$ rounds to $\mathbf{52.7}$.
  • (3) $7.9$ is between $7$ and $8$, and is closer to $8$ (tenths digit is $9 \ge 5$). So $7.9$ rounds to $\mathbf{8}$.
(1) 7 kg | (2) 52.7 | (3) 8 mm

12 A zoo has two baby koala bears named Max and Jake. Max weighs $26.27\text{ pounds}$ and Jake weighs $26.2\text{ pounds}$.
Use the 4-step problem-solving plan and the place-value chart to determine: Which baby koala bear is heavier?
✍️ Student Workspace
✓ Step-by-Step Model Solution 4-Step Problem Solving
  • 1. Read & Understand: Max weighs $26.27\text{ lbs}$ and Jake weighs $26.2\text{ lbs}$. We need to find which koala is heavier.
  • 2. Make a Plan: Line up the decimals in a place-value chart ($26.27$ and $26.20$) and compare digits from left to right.
  • 3. Carry Out the Plan: Tens ($2=2$), Ones ($6=6$), and Tenths ($2=2$) are equal. In the hundredths place, $7 > 0$, so $26.27 > 26.20$.
  • 4. Check: Therefore, Max is heavier than Jake.
26.27 > 26.20 | Max is heavier
